How mileage and cost are worked out

And what the numbers do not include

Miles

Road distance from the routing engine, not straight lines. The drive out from the warehouse plus every leg between stops. It ends at the last delivery, because that is where the block ends. Nothing here assumes a drive back.

Gas

Miles divided by the MPG for that vehicle class, times the fuel price. This is the money that actually leaves the driver's pocket on the day. A truck doing 17 will cost noticeably more over the same route than a sedan doing 32, which is worth seeing before you decide who takes which route.

All-in

A separate, larger figure: miles times a per-mile rate meant to cover everything the driving costs, not just gas. Tyres, servicing, and the value the vehicle loses by being driven. The default is the IRS business mileage rate.

The two are not additive. All-in already includes the gas. They sit side by side because they answer different questions: gas is what today costs, all-in is closer to what the miles are really worth.

Why there is no fleet total

The useful comparison is one route against another, so you can see which driver drew the expensive run and move work off it. A total across the fleet hides exactly that. Every figure here is per route.

What is left out

Driver pay, tolls and parking. Also any drive home after the last stop, which is the driver's own trip and nothing to do with the block. These are estimates for comparing routes, not accounting figures.

How the savings are measured

Two comparisons, two different questions

What it compares

The baseline is what you would get with no software: take the manifest top to bottom, split it into equal piles, one per driver, and send everyone out. Each pile is then sequenced the same way a real route is, since the Flex app would reorder the stops anyway. That removes stop ordering from the comparison entirely.

Why the headline is a problem count, not a percentage

Manifests usually arrive sorted by tracking or ZIP, and ZIPs are already roughly geographic, so chopping one into even piles can land surprisingly close on raw drive time. Judging the plan on drive time alone would therefore flatter the baseline.

The catch is that those piles are not dispatchable. Nothing checks whether a pile fits the block, fits the vehicle, or contains an item too long for the trunk. The headline counts how many of those piles would fail before dispatch. This plan is built to score zero, and the drive time is reported alongside so you can see both.

Like for like

Only stops that actually got assigned are counted. If packages ended up unassigned, the baseline is built from the same delivered stops, so the plan gets no credit for skipping work it could not fit.

What is left out

Drive time only. Service time is excluded, since handing over packages takes the same time no matter how the route was planned.

Routes are still internally resequenced with 2-opt before the drive time is measured. That keeps the time estimates, ETAs and constraint checks realistic. It is simply not counted as a saving, because the Flex app would reorder the stops anyway.
